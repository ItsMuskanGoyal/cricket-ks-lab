Kinematic sequence Watch
What happened
Firing order: Pelvis → Torso → Arm → Hands. Your torso was still at 92% of its peak when the arm peaked - it didn't slow down to pass its speed on. Your arm was still at 94% of its peak when the hands peaked - it didn't slow down to pass its speed on.
Why it matters
In an efficient swing each body segment reaches its peak speed just after the one below it, from the inside out, so speed is amplified link by link (golf measures this with K-Vest). Out-of-order peaks or speed drops are where power leaks.

What happened
Your wrists released (uncocked) about 47% of the way through the downswing. The lead arm reached peak speed before the hands, so the shoulder led the swing.
Why it matters
Leading with the shoulder and releasing the wrists late keeps the bat face stable and adds speed at the right moment; early 'flicking' wrists lose control and power.

Honesty box

These are estimates from a single 2D video. Angles and speeds come from Google MediaPipe pose landmarks, filtered with a zero-lag low-pass filter; depth is refined using constant bone lengths. Verdict thresholds are heuristics, not elite norms. Skeleton calibrated to your height + standard body proportions (scale ×0.92, height 172 cm).

What one camera can't measure well

  • Depth (towards / away from the camera): One camera sees across and up/down; depth comes from a learned estimate, so rotations of a side-on body are approximations.
  • Bat and ball: The bat isn't tracked: the hands are used as a proxy, so true bat speed and bat-face angle are unknown. Impact is estimated from peak hand speed.
  • Forces and weight transfer: Without force plates we can't see how hard each foot pushes or how weight shifts.
  • Eyes: Where the eyes look (and when they track the ball) needs eye tracking, not a body camera.
  • Fine timing: At 30 fps each frame is 33 ms - about the same as the gaps between segment peaks. Slow-motion helps a lot.

What the full system adds

  • Real 3D rotation: multi-angle high-speed cameras fitted to the player's calibrated digital twin
  • Force and weight transfer: force plates in the lab session, learned into match-day estimates
  • Eye tracking: where and when the batter watches the ball
  • Exact bat speed and path: bat sensors in the lab and a bat-keypoint model on match video
  • Player-specific baselines: technique health compared with the player's own best, across matches, matchups and workload
